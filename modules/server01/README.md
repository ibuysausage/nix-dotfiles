# 12 Number
**12** (**twelve**) is the [natural number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_number "Natural number") following [11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_\(number\) "11 (number)") and preceding [13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13_\(number\) "13 (number)").

Twelve is the 3rd [superior highly composite number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_highly_composite_number "Superior highly composite number"), the 3rd [colossally abundant number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossally_abundant_number "Colossally abundant number"), the 5th [highly composite number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highly_composite_number "Highly composite number"), and is divisible by the numbers from [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1_\(number\) "1 (number)") to [4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_\(number\) "4 (number)"), and [6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6_\(number\) "6 (number)"), a comparatively large number of divisors.

It is central to many systems of [timekeeping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time "Time"), including the [Western calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar "Gregorian calendar") and units of time of day, and frequently appears in the world's [major religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_religious_groups "Major religious groups").

## Name

Twelve is the largest number with a [single-syllable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monosyllable "Monosyllable") name in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language "English language"). Early [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages "Germanic languages") numbers have been theorized to have been non-[decimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal "Decimal"): evidence includes the unusual phrasing of [eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11_\(number\) "11 (number)") and twelve, the [former use of "hundred"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_hundred "Long hundred") to refer to groups of [120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/120_\(number\) "120 (number)"), and the presence of glosses such as "tentywise" or "ten-count" in medieval texts showing that writers could not presume their readers would normally understand them that way.[\[1\]](#cite_note-1)[\[2\]](#cite_note-2)[\[3\]](#cite_note-Goodare-3) Such uses gradually disappeared with the introduction of [Arabic numerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_numerals "Arabic numerals") during the [12th-century Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th-century_Renaissance "12th-century Renaissance").

Derived from [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English "Old English"), **twelf** and **tuelf** are first attested in the 10th-century [Lindisfarne Gospels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindisfarne_Gospels "Lindisfarne Gospels")' [Book of John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_John "Book of John").[\[note 1\]](#cite_note-5)[\[5\]](#cite_note-oed-6) It has cognates in every [Germanic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_language "Germanic language") (e.g. German *zwölf*), whose [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language "Proto-Germanic language") ancestor has been [reconstructed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic_reconstruction "Linguistic reconstruction") as *\**twaliƀi...**, from *\**twa** ("[two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_\(number\) "2 (number)")") and suffix *\**\-lif-** or *\**\-liƀ-** of uncertain meaning.[\[5\]](#cite_note-oed-6) It is sometimes compared with the [Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language "Lithuanian language") **dvýlika**, although **\-lika** is used as the suffix for all numbers from 11 to 19 (analogous to "-teen").[\[5\]](#cite_note-oed-6) Every other [Indo-European language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_language "Indo-European language") instead uses a form of "two"+"[ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10_\(number\) "10 (number)")", such as the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") **duōdecim**.[\[5\]](#cite_note-oed-6) The usual [ordinal form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_number "Ordinal number") is "twelfth" but "dozenth" or "duodecimal" (from the Latin word) is also used in some contexts, particularly [base-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base-12 "Base-12") numeration. Similarly, a group of twelve things is usually a "[dozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dozen "Dozen")" but may also be referred to as a "dodecad" or "duodecad". The adjective referring to a group of twelve is "duodecuple".

As with eleven,[\[6\]](#cite_note-oedgoesto-7) the earliest forms of twelve are often considered to be connected with Proto-Germanic *\**liƀan** or *\**liƀan** ("to leave"), with the implicit meaning that "two is left" after having already counted to ten.[\[5\]](#cite_note-oed-6) The Lithuanian suffix is also considered to share a similar development.[\[5\]](#cite_note-oed-6) The suffix *\**\-lif-** has also been connected with reconstructions of the Proto-Germanic for ten.[\[6\]](#cite_note-oedgoesto-7)[\[7\]](#cite_note-8)

As mentioned above, 12 has its own name in Germanic languages such as English ([dozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dozen "Dozen")), Dutch (*dozijn*), German (*Dutzend*), and Swedish (*dussin*), all derived from Old French **dozaine**. It is a compound number in many other languages, e.g. Italian **dodici** (but in Spanish and Portuguese, 16, and in French, 17 is the first compound number), Japanese 十二 *jūni*.

### Written representation

In prose writing, *twelve*, being the last single-syllable numeral, is sometimes taken as the last number to be written as a word, and [13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/13_\(number\) "13 (number)") the first to be written using digits. This is not a binding rule, and in English language tradition, it is sometimes recommended to spell out numbers up to and including either *nine*, *ten* or *twelve*, or even *ninety-nine* or *one hundred*. Another system spells out all numbers written in one or two words (*sixteen*, *twenty-seven*, *fifteen thousand*, but *372* or *15,001*).[\[8\]](#cite_note-9) In [German orthography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_orthography "German orthography"), there used to be the widely followed (but unofficial) rule of spelling out numbers up to twelve (*zwölf*). The [Duden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duden "Duden") (the German standard dictionary) mentions this rule as outdated.

## In mathematics

### Properties

12 is the smallest [abundant number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abundant_number "Abundant number") because the sum of the [proper divisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proper_divisors "Proper divisors") of 12, 16, is greater than 12. It is also the 3rd [colossally abundant number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossally_abundant_number "Colossally abundant number").[\[9\]](#cite_note-10) 12 is a [semiperfect number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semiperfect_number "Semiperfect number") because some of its proper divisors; 1, 2, 3, and 6; sum to 12.[\[10\]](#cite_note-11)

12 is a [highly composite number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highly_composite_number "Highly composite number") because it has 6 divisors, more than any number less than 12.[\[11\]](#cite_note-12) It is the 3rd [superior highly composite number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_highly_composite_number "Superior highly composite number").[\[12\]](#cite_note-13) 12 is a [refactorable number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refactorable_number "Refactorable number") because it has 6 divisors and 12 is a multiple of 6.[\[13\]](#cite_note-14) It is the smallest of two known [sublime numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublime_number "Sublime number"), numbers that have a [perfect number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_number "Perfect number") of divisors whose sum is also perfect.[\[14\]](#cite_note-15)

12 is a [Pell number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pell_number "Pell number") because 17/12⁠⁠ is the closest approximation of the square root of 2 of any fraction with a denominator less than or equal to 12.[\[15\]](#cite_note-16)

There are twelve [Jacobian elliptic functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobian_elliptic_function "Jacobian elliptic function") and twelve cubic [distance-transitive graphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance-transitive_graph "Distance-transitive graph").[\[16\]](#cite_note-17)[\[17\]](#cite_note-18)

### Shapes

A twelve-sided [polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polygon "Polygon") is a [dodecagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecagon "Dodecagon"). In its regular form, it is the largest polygon that can [uniformly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclidean_tilings_by_convex_regular_polygons#Archimedean,_uniform_or_semiregular_tilings "Euclidean tilings by convex regular polygons") tile the plane alongside other regular polygons, as with the [truncated hexagonal tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_hexagonal_tiling "Truncated hexagonal tiling") or the [truncated trihexagonal tiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truncated_trihexagonal_tiling "Truncated trihexagonal tiling").[\[18\]](#cite_note-19)

A regular [dodecahedron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_dodecahedron "Regular dodecahedron") has twelve [pentagonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regular_pentagon "Regular pentagon") faces. Regular [cubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube "Cube") and [octahedrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octahedron "Octahedron") both have 12 edges, while regular [icosahedrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedron "Icosahedron") have 12 vertices.

The cubic close packing and hexagonal close packing, which are the two densest possible [sphere packings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_packing "Sphere packing") in three-dimensional space (the [Kepler conjecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler_conjecture "Kepler conjecture"), proved by [Thomas Hales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Callister_Hales "Thomas Callister Hales")), both have each sphere touching twelve other spheres. Twelve is also the [kissing number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kissing_number "Kissing number") in three dimensions.

There are twelve [complex apeirotopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_polytope#Regular_complex_5-apeirotopes_and_higher "Complex polytope") in dimensions five and higher, which include [van Oss polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_polytope#van_Oss_polygon "Complex polytope") in the form of complex \-[orthoplexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthoplex "Orthoplex").[\[19\]](#cite_note-20) There are also twelve [paracompact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_6-polytope#Regular_and_uniform_hyperbolic_honeycombs "Uniform 6-polytope") hyperbolic [Coxeter groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coxeter_group "Coxeter group") of [uniform polytopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_polytope "Uniform polytope") in five-dimensional space.

[Bring's curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bring's_curve "Bring's curve") is a [Riemann surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_surface "Riemann surface") of [genus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus_\(mathematics\) "Genus (mathematics)") four, with a domain that is a regular hyperbolic 20-sided [icosagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosagon "Icosagon").[\[20\]](#cite_note-21) By the [Gauss-Bonnet theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauss-Bonnet_theorem "Gauss-Bonnet theorem"), the area of this [fundamental polygon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_polygon "Fundamental polygon") is equal to .

#### In other bases

The [duodecimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duodecimal "Duodecimal") system (1210 \[twelve\] = 1012), which is the use of 12 as a division factor for many [ancient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_weights_and_measures "Ancient weights and measures") and [medieval weights and measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_weights_and_measures "Medieval weights and measures"), including [hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hour "Hour"), probably originates from [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia "Mesopotamia").

## Religion

The number twelve carries religious, mythological and magical [symbolism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symbol "Symbol"); since antiquity, the number has generally represented perfection, entirety, or cosmic order.[\[21\]](#cite_note-FOOTNOTEDrews1972p._43,_n._10-22)

The number 12 is notable within the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible "Hebrew Bible"), and in Christianity:

[Ishmael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishmael "Ishmael")—the first-born son of [Abraham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham "Abraham")—has 12 sons/princes ([Genesis 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chayei_Sarah "Chayei Sarah"):16), and [Jacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob "Jacob") also has 12 sons, who are the progenitors of the [Twelve Tribes of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Tribes_of_Israel "Twelve Tribes of Israel").[\[22\]](#cite_note-23) This is reflected in Christian tradition, notably in the [twelve Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Apostles "Twelve Apostles"). When [Judas Iscariot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_Iscariot "Judas Iscariot") is disgraced, a meeting is held ([Acts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acts_of_the_Apostles "Acts of the Apostles")) to add [Saint Matthias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Matthias "Saint Matthias") to complete the number twelve once more.

The Old Testament contains [Twelve Minor Prophets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Minor_Prophets "Twelve Minor Prophets").

The [Book of Revelation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Revelation "Book of Revelation") contains much numerical symbolism, and many of the numbers mentioned have 12 as a divisor. [12:1](https://www.biblica.com/bible/?osis=niv:Revelation%2012:1) mentions a woman—interpreted as the [people of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites "Israelites"), the [Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church "Christian Church") and the [Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blessed_Virgin_Mary "Blessed Virgin Mary")—wearing a crown of twelve stars (representing each of the twelve tribes of Israel). Furthermore, there are 12,000 people sealed from each of the twelve tribes of Israel (the [Tribe of Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Dan "Tribe of Dan") is omitted while [Manasseh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Joseph "Tribe of Joseph") is mentioned), making a total of [144,000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/144000_\(number\) "144000 (number)") (which is the square of 12 multiplied by a thousand).

- According to the New Testament, Jesus had [twelve Apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve_Apostles "Twelve Apostles").
- The "[Twelve Days of Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_Night_\(holiday\) "Twelfth Night (holiday)")" count the interval between [Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas "Christmas") and [Epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_\(feast\) "Epiphany (feast)").

## Timekeeping

- The [lunar year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_year "Lunar year") is 12 [lunar months](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_months "Lunar months"). Adding 11 or 12 days completes the [solar year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_year "Solar year").[\[26\]](#cite_note-27)
- Most [calendar systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar "Calendar") – solar or lunar – have twelve months in a [year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year "Year").
- The Chinese use a 12-year cycle for time-reckoning called [Earthly Branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthly_Branches "Earthly Branches").
- There are twelve [hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hour "Hour") in a half day, numbered one to twelve for both the *ante meridiem* (a.m.) and the *post meridiem* (p.m.). 12:00 p.m. is midday or [noon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noon "Noon"), and 12:00 a.m. is [midnight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight "Midnight").
- The basic units of time (60 seconds, 60 minutes, 24 hours) are evenly divisible by twelve into smaller units.

### Music

#### Music theory

- Twelve is the number of [pitch classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitch_class "Pitch class") in an [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave "Octave"), not counting the duplicated (octave) pitch. Also, the total number of [major keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_key "Major key"), (not counting [enharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enharmonic "Enharmonic") equivalents) and the total number of [minor keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_key "Minor key") (also not counting equivalents). This applies only to twelve tone [equal temperament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equal_temperament "Equal temperament"), the most common tuning used today in western influenced music.
- The twelfth is the [interval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_\(music\) "Interval (music)") of an octave and a fifth. Instruments such as the [clarinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarinet "Clarinet") which behave as a stopped cylindrical pipe [overblow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overblowing "Overblowing") at the twelfth.
- The [twelve-tone technique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-tone_technique "Twelve-tone technique") (also dodecaphony) is a method of [musical composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_composition "Musical composition") devised by [Arnold Schoenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schoenberg "Arnold Schoenberg"). Music using the technique is called twelve-tone music.
- The [twelve-bar blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-bar_blues "Twelve-bar blues") is one of the most prominent chord progressions in popular music.

### Art theory

- There are twelve basic [hues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hue "Hue") in the [color wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_wheel "Color wheel"): three primary colors (red, yellow, blue), three secondary colors (orange, green, purple) and six tertiary colors (names for these vary, but are intermediates between the primaries and secondaries).
